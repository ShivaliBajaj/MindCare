MindCare Taxonomy v1

Purpose

MindCare uses a clinically informed behavioral signal taxonomy to identify and interpret meaningful patterns in mental health related text. The taxonomy is intended to support explainable behavioral assessment and does not provide diagnosis or clinical treatment recommendations.

⸻

Distress

Definition

Expressions of emotional suffering, overwhelm, agitation, psychological pain, or difficulty coping.

Example Expressions

* “I can’t handle this anymore.”
* “Everything feels overwhelming.”

Clinical Relevance

Distress may indicate significant psychological strain and is an important contextual signal when evaluated alongside other behavioral indicators.

⸻

Hopelessness

Definition

Expressions suggesting a negative outlook toward the future or a belief that circumstances cannot improve.

Example Expressions

* “Nothing will ever get better.”
* “There is no point trying anymore.”

Clinical Relevance

Hopelessness is frequently discussed in mental health literature as an important behavioral risk signal.

⸻

Isolation

Definition

Expressions of loneliness, social withdrawal, disconnection, or lack of meaningful support.

Example Expressions

* “Nobody understands me.”
* “I feel completely alone.”

Clinical Relevance

Isolation may contribute to psychological vulnerability and can amplify the significance of other behavioral signals.

⸻

Burdensomeness

Definition

Expressions suggesting that an individual believes they are a burden to others.

Example Expressions

* “Everyone would be better off without me.”
* “I only cause problems.”

Clinical Relevance

Perceived burdensomeness is commonly discussed in suicide-related research and may be important when observed alongside other signals.

⸻

Help-Seeking

Definition

Expressions indicating a desire for support, guidance, assistance, or intervention.

Example Expressions

* “I need help.”
* “Can someone talk to me?”

Clinical Relevance

Help-seeking is treated as a protective factor within MindCare and may reduce overall concern when present.

⸻

Suicidal Ideation

Definition

Expressions involving thoughts, consideration, or discussion of self-harm or suicide.

Example Expressions

* “I think about ending my life.”
* “I don’t want to be here anymore.”

Clinical Relevance

Suicidal ideation is a critical behavioral signal requiring careful interpretation within broader context.

⸻

Escalation

Definition

A temporal increase in behavioral concern observed across multiple interactions over time.

Example Expressions

Not derived from a single statement. Escalation is identified through changes in behavioral signals across a sequence of observations.

Clinical Relevance

Escalation helps identify worsening behavioral patterns and supports temporal risk assessment.
